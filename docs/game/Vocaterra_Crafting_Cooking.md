Alter Vocaterra so that cooking and crafting are two concrete examples of a broader design principle: normal gameplay systems should be deep enough to create repeated, contextual language practice without needing endless unique quests or NPCs.

Cooking should be an in-depth system based on many real-life recipes. The player should gather ingredients, select cookware and utensils such as pans, bowls, knives, spatulas, and whisks, and then carry out the recipe step by step. It should support optional guided walkthroughs, especially for beginners, where instructions can highlight the correct item or tool and provide Spanish text, audio, and translation. Over time, guidance should reduce from full support, to Spanish-only instructions, to spoken instructions, and eventually to higher-level recipe steps that the player completes independently. This naturally reinforces vocabulary for ingredients, kitchen objects, actions such as cut, mix, pour, whisk, heat, and stir, as well as quantities and sequencing.

Crafting should follow the same philosophy and be more procedural than simple Minecraft-style instant recipes. Instead of combining a few items in a grid, the player should use actual tools, materials, and ordered assembly steps. For example, crafting an axe might involve selecting wood and iron, shaping components, positioning pieces, attaching the head to the handle, and using tools such as a hammer. This creates repeated exposure to vocabulary for materials, tools, object parts, quantities, positioning, and verbs such as cut, attach, place, turn, hold, and hit. Guidance should also reduce over time in the same way as cooking.

Use cooking and crafting as reference systems when reviewing the rest of Vocaterra. Look for other existing or future systems such as farming, building, trading, exploration, gathering, repairs, or survival that could be made sufficiently interactive to naturally reinforce the same language across different contexts. The aim is for the game simulation itself to provide repetition and language practice, rather than depending primarily on bespoke language-learning quests.
