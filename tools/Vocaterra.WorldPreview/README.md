# Vocaterra.WorldPreview

Draws the procedural world as PNG pictures (B02, `docs/game/biomes-plan.md`): the biome of every column, the ground's height, and a vertical slice. It loads `content/game-v1` through `GameData`, reads B01's climate (`ClimateSampler`, filled in strips with `Fill` at 4 blocks a pixel or finer), height model (`HeightModel`) and biome table (`BiomeTable.Classify`), and changes nothing: no tuning, no content edit. The PNGs are written by hand (`ZLibStream` and a CRC-32), so the tool takes no package.

```bash
# both maps of one area (the default area is 4 096 x 4 096 blocks centred on 0,0, 4 blocks a pixel)
scripts/limited.sh dotnet run --project tools/Vocaterra.WorldPreview -- maps --seed 42 --out artifacts/b-series/b02
# one map, a larger area
scripts/limited.sh dotnet run --project tools/Vocaterra.WorldPreview -- biomes --seed 7 --x 0 --z 0 --size 16384 --scale 16 --out biomes.png
scripts/limited.sh dotnet run --project tools/Vocaterra.WorldPreview -- heights --seed 7 --size 16384 --scale 16 --out artifacts/b-series/b02
# a vertical section along a line, 2 pixels per block of height
scripts/limited.sh dotnet run --project tools/Vocaterra.WorldPreview -- slice --seed 42 --x0 2560 --z0 744 --x1 6656 --z1 744 --scale 4 --out slice.png
```

| Command | Writes |
|---|---|
| `biomes` | each column in its biome row's colour; `<name>.legend.txt` lists each row's id, colour and share of the area |
| `heights` | sea blue by depth, lowland green to upland brown to grey stone, white where snow lies (where the biome table says snow covers the column: the row allows it and the surface temperature is below the threshold), lit from the north-west; `<name>.stats.txt` gives the lowest and highest ground and the share of land above 100, 150, 200 and 250 blocks |
| `maps` | both, for the same area |
| `stats` | no picture: the owner's map-review numbers (B02b) as text and, with `--out`, as a file: the water share, the sea (biome `ocean`) and its connectivity (bodies, the largest's share of the sea, the five largest), the share of the land above 150, 200 and 250, the lowest snow-covered ground, the rivers (bodies of columns under water that a river carved, and how many reach other water, the sea or a lake; a body that runs off the map is left out), and the coast profile (the mean ground by distance from the shore, on the land and out to sea). Round 3 adds: the river line (the columns carved 0.9 or more) below the height rivers fade out from, and the share of them under water with the number of different water levels; the ranges (the eight-connected bodies of land above 150 and above 180: the length and width from the spread of the body, and its thickness, 2 x area / perimeter, which is the width of a strip however it forks; the share of the area in chains, thinner than 500 blocks and 4 times as long, and in massifs); the river humps (the reaches of river water higher than every reach they touch on two sides, neither of which falls into the other: the line rises and falls again; their share of the river water and the largest rise, and where the first are), and B02c's `network:` line, read straight from the model: the reaches and their length, the sources (per 16 384-block square), the reaches that end in the sea or a lake, how often the water rises along a reach and along a chain of reaches (the rule: never), the share of the river's length whose chain ends in the sea or a lake, and the share of the land within 500 blocks of a river, and the straightness of the coast (the longest run along which a coast column lies within a column of a straight line, and the share of the coast on a run of 300 blocks or more) It names the biome ids `ocean` and `lake-basin` |
| `slice` | y -64 to 320: ground in the surface biome's colour, water blue, the snow line (white) and tree line (green), the sea level dashed yellow, ticks every 64 blocks up and every 1 024 along; with `--mode blocks` (B11) the real blocks of the world, each in its own colour (stone grey, slate dark blue, granite pink, limestone cream, basalt charcoal, mossy stone green, cobble light grey, gravel, sand and dirt in their own tints, bedrock black, lava orange, water blue, iron, tin and copper ore in their own tints), so the strata, the ores, the caves, the lava, the floor and the entrances show |
| `river` | B02c: a river of the drainage network followed from a point on it (`--x --z`, `--length` blocks, `--step` blocks a sample, `--direction down` from the point to the sea, `up` from the source to the point, or `both`, the whole river, the default): `HeightModel.TraceRiver` finds the river through the nearest node and the preview walks its line, taking the column of the most carve near each sample, and the ground (brown), the natural ground (a thin grey line), the water (blue) and the sea level (dashed yellow) along it are drawn as one section, source on the left: the water steps down and never rises; `<name>.txt` says how far it went, the share under water, the number of steps, the length of the reaches, the height of the steps and the **rises along the flow (there must be none)** |
| `drainage` | B02c: the heights map with the drainage network drawn over it: every reach of river as a line from node to node, thicker the more ground drains into it (1 to 4 pixels); `--basin-x --basin-z` (a point on a river) draws that river's whole basin, the river and every tributary to the sea or lake it ends in, in orange and the rest in blue; it prints the six largest rivers' last reaches (x, z, nodes draining, what they end in) |
| `entrances` | B11, B11b: the cave entrances of a square area, one pixel a block (`--size` at most 2 048): the ground in grey by height, water blue, each passage in orange with its mouth in red; it prints how many it found and lists each (the mouth, the ground there, the cells along its path to its cave, how far down that is, the ground over its first stretch) with stops along the path (cell, column and floor) for placing a camera |

`--world valley` (B06) samples the world as the streamed game makes it round the valley, through the scene's place query (`Scene.Places`): the valley's own ground inside its footprint, the land eased into its edge across the blend margin, the continentalness pulled towards land. Its maps carry the outlines of the footprint (red), the end of the blend margin (orange) and the land bias's radius (dashed yellow); `--world free` (the default) is the procedural world alone.

`--region <layout.json> --region-x <x> --region-z <z> [--region-layer <n>]` (B15) builds a town from a layout file on the procedural ground of the seed (its first column at the block, rounded down to a chunk; its first chunk layer by `ProceduralReliefRegion.Fit` unless given) and draws the maps and slices of the streamed world with it (`--world valley` is implied); its footprint and margin are outlined like the valley's, and the files are named `town-...`.

Options: `--world` (`free` or `valley`), `--seed` (default 42), `--x` `--z` (the area's centre), `--size` (blocks, default 4 096), `--scale` (blocks a pixel, default 4; the size is a multiple of it), `--out` (a folder, or a `.png` path for one picture; files in a folder are named from the seed, centre, size and scale), `--content` (default: `content/game-v1` of the checkout), `--threads` (default 4), and for `slice` `--x0 --z0 --x1 --z1`, `--zoom` (pixels per block of height, default 2). x runs to the right, z down the picture. The same arguments write byte-identical files. A 4 096-block map at 4 blocks a pixel, and a 16 384-block one at 16, each take about a second after the bundle loads.

The palette lives in `Palette.cs`: a hue from each row's position in `biomes.json` (the golden-ratio step), and blues for rows whose `when` asks for water. No biome id is written in the code. Tests: `tests/Vocaterra.WorldPreview.Tests` (CRC-32 vectors, PNG decoding, byte-identical output, the biome map against `BiomeTable.Classify`).
